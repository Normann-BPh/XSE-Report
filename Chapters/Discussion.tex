\section*{Discussion}
This report focuses on not a decisive evaluation of the generalized applicability of this algorithm-collection, but the case-specific use on \textsc{Xenium} and FFPE-Spleen data. The evaluation is limited in its merit, chiefly rooted in the necessity of a ground-truth and its apparent quality. The custom-pipeline, developed in tandem, though fully functional, has yet to be tested by unfamiliar users.\\
% - obvious limitations of the algortihms AND the evaluation methods.\\
The reference-based analysis, under consideration of the visual interpretation, showed a clear dominance of \textsc{XOA}. The prerequisite to call upon a human assessment exemplifies the inconsistency of reference-based evaluations. The quality of \textsc{XOA}s prediction can with certainty be attributed to the, as described in the introduction, \textsc{Xenium}-output exclusive training.\\
From the reference-based evaluations we can also assess that the \emph{ground-truth}, as it aligns more with the nucleus-focused predictions \textsc{StarDist} and \textsc{XOA} --- though not \textsc{segger} ---, represents a nucleus- and not a cell-segmentation. This impacts the validity of the evaluation of pure-cell-segmentations like \textsc{DINOCell}.\\
Considering the aforementioned and the, albeit presently untrialled, human-surpassing potential of computer-vision, recent reference-free strategies appear as exclusive avenues for future developments in evaluation and segmentation-training. The \inlineprogram{CSE} package stands as an evident candidate, but is severely restrained by panel-composition and complexity. Extending this approach to include SRT recordings could not only broaden the pertinence but could, as some methods provide 3-dimensionally resolved data, improve on the \emph{cell-shape-prioritisation}. Though highly relevant to correct cell identification, and clearly penalised in conventional evaluation methods, this feature is left unattended by \inlineprogram{CSE}.\\
The cross-evaluation presented in this report is based on the expectation, that if there are multiple algorithms that perform well they should agree with one another, but must not necessarily with the ground-truth. This has been observed as \textsc{ProSeg} shows higher similarity with \textsc{StarDist} and \textsc{XOA} than with the \emph{ground-truth}. This observation emphasises \textsc{ProSeg}s struggle with high-density transcripts-accumulation --- possibly mitigated through segmentation-priors. I suggest to constrain the application of cross-evaluation to identifying possible candidates for 3D-segmentation tasks, as they should explain throughout all layers a consistent tissue. This consistency is present in \textsc{ProSeg}, thus a contender for \textsc{Xenium}-based 3D-analysis. Since no other model that could be adapted for 3D-segmentation --- \textsc{CPSAM, DINOCell, DISSECT} --- performs as well, developing strategies around \textsc{ProSeg} is advisable.\\
