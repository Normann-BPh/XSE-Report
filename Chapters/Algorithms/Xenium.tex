\subsection*{\color{xenium}\textsc{Xenium Onboard Analysis}}
Xeniums Onboard Anaylsis (\textsc{xoa}) provides nucleus and cell boundaries, predicted utilizing the DAPI, 18S and Protein stains, and custom neural networks. The neural network is described as a similar approach to the Cellpose architecture, in that an encoder-decoder pair predicts not masks but a geometric sub-problem. It is trained exclusively on a multiplicity of Xenium analysed tissues. \cite{janesick_high_2023} \cite{noauthor_nucleus_nodate} \cite{noauthor_understanding_nodate}\\
Solely the DAPI stain informs the deep-neural-network on nuclei position and shape. The cells are then either 1) predicted by expanding the nucleus-bounds by 5$\unit{\micro\meter}$ or until it meets another cells boundary, or, 2) if the boundary stains are present, applying, again, a multimodal algorithm to infer cell boundaries. Alternatively, if apart from the DAPI stain only 18s RNA stains are available, the nucleus is expanded until this stain ends, derived by the \textit{custom neural network}, or a different cell is meet. Neither paper nor the support page further explain the architecture of the model, its training method, or factors influencing cell-segmentation mode. The interior protein stain, alphaSMA/Vimentin, is currently not in use, since the 18S marker performs adequately, according to \textsc{10XGenomics}\cite{noauthor_nucleus_nodate}.
