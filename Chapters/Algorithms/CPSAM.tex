\subsection*{\color{cpsam}\textsc{Cellpose-SAM}}
Cellpose-SAM (CPSAM), essentially the fourth iteration of Cellpose, is a widely used algorithm utilizing a generalized model as its backbone. It replaces the Cellpose U-net backbone with the foundational model SAM \cite{kirillov_segment_2023}. This new model achieves \textit{superhuman generalization}, i.e. breaking the inter-annotator bound \cite{pachitariu_cellpose-sam_2025}.\\
The backbone of the first Cellpose, a U-Net model, predicts vector flows, an intermediate pixel-wise representation of the mask that point towards the cell-center \cite{stringer_cellpose_2021}. This sub-problem, similar to gradient-flow based segmentation \cite{li_segmentation_2008}, has shown to improve complex cell shape prediction \cite{stringer_cellpose_2021}. The SAM ViT-L encoder is used as a backbone to replace the U-Net model, custom modifications --- input and patch size, and global attention --- allow the utilization of the SAM-weights to predict the vectorized flow representation. A majority of the SAM parameters were kept (305M out of 312M) enabling the use of its generalized understanding of images.\\
As input the CPSAM permits single- or multi-layer, multi-channel images. CPSAM, trained for channel-order invariance, accepts up to 3 channels, but requires a specific input shape, $n_{planes} \times n_{channels} \times n_{Y} \times n_{X}$ . However, cellular image-prediction was exclusively trained on cytoplasm and nucleus staining, while the third channel was set to zero.\\
The received output is in a structured array, including among others the cell-masks as a pixel map.
