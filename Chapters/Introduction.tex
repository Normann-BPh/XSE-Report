\subsection*{Benchmarking}
Benchmarking segmentation is not constraint to just cell segmentation - medical imagining including MRI or CT | or financial data | can be evaluated with the same or similar methods. F1 scores stand as the deafult value. investiagting the Precision and Recall seperately can yield additional insights but usually i don't get why people add it to the tables... \\
The typical setup is to draw segmentation masks from the ground up as a ground truth. this can be a one person job or go through multiple ronds of verification or be crowd-sourced as in \textsc{TissueNet}. these masks are then compared to the prediction and assigned a numerical value - QUANTIFIED - by calculating the Intersection over Union (IoU), also called Jaccard(-Value), which, with a set threshold, can be used to classify the predictions as either true or false from which we conclude the precision and recal. \\
Virtually all benchmarks and methods/numeric values are based on this process. (human made masks vs prediction). In some machine learning cases, namely LLMs, it was suggested to rely primarily on machine-jury/-annotators for benchmarks. This is of course not entirely applicable to medical or cell-cegmentatin. other maskless attempts have been made to deal with the increasing aptness of ML-segmentation. PCA by CellSegmentationEvaluation, relies on metrics that apply to both the staining-image and the prediction and comparse them both. the limits will be shown later. another approach is probability based, by using multiple algorithms to predict and accumulate their results in a probability map we can evaluate an algorithm without a human-annotator. \\
