Evaluation of segmentation algorithms has long been done by comparing the cell-predictions to a ground truth, masks drawn by humans, usually professionals in the respective field --- DeepCell and the TissueNet-project crowd-sourced supervised training. The human-centered approach has many short-comings, evident is the human labour required for manual segmentation. Further, the reliability of these segmentations. Distinguishing between cell, nucleus, extra-cellular-matrix, and other structures, is often a heuristic task. Nonetheless, the quality of human semantic-segmentation exceeded computer vision in the past, and in some cases continues to, though we, as humans, experience limitations promptly. Especially dense tissues like the spleen. Such compact organs complicate elementary staining techniques, resulting in sub-par membrane and ribosome stains required for unubiquitous segmentation. The tissue available, as seen in Figure \figref{fig:focus} is notably affected by the tissue limitations. Hence, for \textsc{Mesmer}, which requires a two channel-input, a nucleus-channel and a membrane-channel or a cytoplasm-channel, all available staining were tested, including a DAPI-only input, with an empty second-channel.\\
As to not rely on an ambiguous ground-truth I included two additional methods of evaluation. The first is based on principal component analysis (PCA), using the input-image, on which the segmentation was performed, to calculate metrics which indicate a \emph{good} segmentation. The second reference free examination directly compares the cell-predictions to each other --- this of course does not tell which is the closest to a human-identifiable truth, but instead which algorithm provides a stable prediction of the cells.\\

\subsection*{Reference Based}
As seen in \figref{fig:focus} the membrane stain (cyan) is dim, appears to suffer from nonspecifical binding and lacks signal in large regions. This inadequacy is mirrored in the manual segmentation seen in \figref{fig:focus} (bottom left).\\
The test were performed using unet4nuclei's (\inlineprogram{U4N}) evaluation scripts \cite{noauthor_carpenterlab2019_caicedo_cytometrya_2025}, and the \inlineprogram{DeepCell-Toolbox} (\inlineprogram{DT}) package\cite{noauthor_deepcell-toolbox_2023}. \inlineprogram{U4N} solely matches the masks, ground-truth and prediction, to one another and calculates the JI, it then identifies the precision and recall using a user set threshold, and finally calculates the F-score.\\
Conversely, \inlineprogram{DT} constructs a graph connecting each prediction-mask to all ground-truth masks, with the edge representing the JI. If the JI is 1 then this pair is classified as valid. All unmatched predictions are then classified as either as a merge, multiple edges of a single prediction-node have a non-zero JI --- two cells of the ground-truth are merged into a single predicted cell. Or a split, if multiple edges of a GT-cell have a non-zero JI --- the predictions split a single GT-cell.\\
For the \inlineprogram{U4N}-evaluation we see in \figref{fig:u4n_bars} a clear dominance of \textsc{StarDist}, closely followed by \textsc{XOA}s nucleus-segmentation. Both decline less pronounced with increasing threshold. Also observable is the proportional increase of \textsc{DISSECT}, \textsc{ProSeg} and \textbf{segger}, in comparison to the \textsc{CPSAM} and \textsc{Mesmer}. Diametric to \textsc{XOA}, \textsc{segger}s nucleus-prediction scores lower than its cell-prediction. \textsc{ProSeg}s second layer (proseg\us l1) scores higher than its other masks. The DAPI-only prediction of \textsc{Mesmer} (mesmer\us mt) scores considerably higher than other channel-combinations.\\%
The results from the \inlineprogram{DT} analysis, see \figref{fig:dc_bars}, show varying dominance with \textsc{XOA} and \textsc{StarDist} consistently at the higher end. The F1 values map well onto the F1 values of \inlineprogram{U4N} at a threshold of 0.6. The SEG bar-plot presents a nearly invariant score-distribution, indicating proficient cell- and nucleus-shape prediction when considering masks with 50\% overlap with the ground-truth. The JI and DICE scores appear proportional, with similar, but less prominent, distributions as the \inlineprogram{U4N} analysis.\\%

\subsection*{Reference Free}
Since computer vision can theoretically and practically often does outperform manual segmentation and in some cases human-consensus \cite{pachitariu_cellpose-sam_2025}, these manually constructed references are bound to enter their limit in applicability. Methods evaluating based on statistics extracted form the image to segment on \cite{chen_evaluation_2023} or by finding a consensus through probability-aggregates of a mix of segmentation results \cite{sims_seg_2023} are recent attempts to circumvent the manual-masks.
\subsubsection*{CellSegmentationEvaluator}
The CellSegmentationEvaluator (\inlineprogram{CSE}) \cite{chen_evaluation_2023} uses principle component analysis on 11(+3) values that aim to abstract the input-image and the prediction. The values can be categorised into six groups\footnote{refer to the appendix for a complete parameter-name.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item[1)] cells cover most areas (NC)
    \item[2)] cells cover mostly the foreground (FFC, 1-FBC, FCF)
    \item[3)] cells cover nuclei (FMCN)
    \item[4)] homogeneous protein composition (1/(ACFV+1), FPCF)
    \item[5)] homogeneous cell type composition (1/(ACVC+1), FPCC, AS) 
    \item[6)] homogeneous cell size (1/ln(SSD))
\end{itemize}
The here-mentioned cell types were defined using K-Means clustering, performed on the mean cell intensities across all channels, after applying z-score standardization for each channel. This, of course, implies the use of histochemistry with multi-plex panels, the stainings available through \textsc{Xenium} are constrained to DAPI, ATP1A1/CD45/E-Cadherin, 18s rRNA and alphaSMA/Vimentin. The constraint in cell-type identification potentially impeaches the evaluation approach. \inlineprogram{CSE} neither encourages nor penalises uncommon or unnatural cell-shape, as long the proposed protein-composition aligns.\\
\inlineprogram{CSE} also employs a \emph{repair-method}, which corrects nuclei that "leak" out of the cell, or cells which are too small. This repair-method was enabled for this analysis.\\

\subsubsection*{Cross-Evaluation}
Since the confidence in a given ground-truth may be scant, a comparison between the predictions, each acting as the ground-truth and performing a reference-based analysis, can give context to the consistency of predictions. Owing to the equal input one would expect a congruence between well performing segmentations. This evaluation procedure is inspired by the \inlineprogram{SEG} method \cite{sims_seg_2023}, excluding the assumption that a probabilistic combination represents an adequate ground-truth.\\ % to the similarity between model-structure
Consecutively comparing the predictions using IoU-based metrics results in symmetrical outputs, as seen in \figref{fig:results}. The congruency can be observed in (both?) cross-evaluations. the dinocell, mesmer, proseg, stardist and \textsc{XOA} predictions perform well, are congruent among each other. with cpsam, dissect and segger we see that none perform well, indicating that they are, as we see in the gt-based analysis, not good at explaining the tissue. the U4N based values are too low to see any trend. the threshold of 0.65 is probably to high.
