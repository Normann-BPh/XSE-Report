\title{Evaluation of Cell-Segmentation Algorithms on Influenza infected Mouse-Spleen Cells}
%
\author[a,b]{Philomina Lillie Normann}
\affil[a]{Humboldt-Universitaet zu Berlin}
\affil[b]{Berlin Institute of Health (BIH) @ Charite}
% 
\leadauthor{Normann}
% 
\keywords{Python $|$ Cell Segmentation $|$ Evaluation $|$ Xenium $|$ SRT}
% 
\begin{abstract}
    Algorithmic cell segmentation as a growing field has many challenges to face. Recent studies have shown diminished quality in high density tissue. Nonetheless the large datasets generated by spatially resolved transcriptomics cannot be segmented manually, considering the duration of labour and the fact that machine-segmentation statistically outperforms humans. In this report multiple state-of-the-art algorithms were evaluated on Xenium v2 formalin fixed \& parafin embeded spleen samples. The spleen as a large lymphoid organ with important functions in orchestrating immune responses is densely packed with cells, especially in the white pulp regions.
    % Its important immunological functions are key to understanding the adaptive immune system and responses to disease-spread.
    Despite significant advancements in computer vision, almost universal struggle can be observed in predicting cells in the spleen tissue.
    In this work, multiple algorithms --- imaging based, transcript based and using both modalities --- were evaluated. To quantify the algorithms' performance, I used common evaluation metrics: intersection over union, with a manual segmentation as ground-truth, and a reference free approach based on principal component analysis. Additionally, a custom pipeline to prepare, segment and evaluate Xenium v2/v3 output was developed. Of the algorithms evaluated, especially those restricted to imaging data underperformed, whereas the those incorporating transcriptomics data provided more reliable results. Still, both evaluation metrics are created for two-dimensional segmentation, thus making objective evaluation of segmentation results on data sets with large amounts of overlapping cells difficult --- especially in the absence of a three-dimensional ground truth reflecting the cellular overlaps.
    % Methods restricted to imaging data underperform, where as those incorporating spatially resolved transcriptomics improve significantly. To quantify the study I used common evaluation metrics, intersection over union, as well as a reference free approach. Both suggest a limited applicability in evaluating segmentation results. Additionally I present a pipeline to prepare, segment and then evaluate on Xenium v2/v3 output. \\
\end{abstract}
